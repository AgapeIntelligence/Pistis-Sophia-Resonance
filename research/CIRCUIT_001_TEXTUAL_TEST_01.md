## Evidence observed in Chapter 32
1. Narrator introduces Sophia's first repentance as an address to the Light she had trusted from the beginning.
2. Sophia reports seeing a light below and **thinking** she could obtain it. This explicitly separates perception, interpretation/expectation, and subsequent action.
3. She descends into chaos, reports being unable to return, and describes a lion-faced power taking her inner light. The text presents an outcome inconsistent with her initial expectation.
4. Sophia cries for help, reports her voice failing to penetrate darkness, and turns her attention upward.
5. She reports other rulers observing her plight and rejoicing; their perspective is not her own. These are claims in Sophia's narrated speech, not independently established measurements.

## Observer-relative model (interpretive, not textual fact)
- E1: Sophia perceives lower light.
- I1: Sophia interprets it as something to approach and acquire.
- A1: Sophia moves toward it.
- O1: Harm and loss of agency follow.
- E2: Sophia looks upward and petitions a prior trusted referent.

Preserve separately: Sophia's first-person testimony; Jesus's reported narration of Sophia; disciples' subsequent interpretive responses; modern translation; present-day researcher.

## Critical refinement
The text supports a **change in attention and petition after an adverse outcome**. It does **not yet establish** that Sophia recognizes the lower light as a deliberate deception in the first repentance itself, nor that thirteen repentances represent thirteen mathematically distinct correction cycles. Those require testing against the entire sequence and original Coptic.

## Cross-check at Chapter 57
The thirteenth repentance explicitly relates the twelve earlier repentances to twelve aeons and invokes the thirteenth aeon. This is evidence for a structured count, but not sufficient evidence for a resonance or phase-locking mechanism.
