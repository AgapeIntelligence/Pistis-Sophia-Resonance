## Observer-related textual anchors
**Chapter 32:** Sophia sees a lower light, interprets it as obtainable, descends and becomes trapped; later explicitly describes believing the lion-faced light-power belonged to the higher Light. This is stronger evidence of *misattribution* than the earlier working note acknowledged. She reports rulers watching and rejoicing: keep reported observation separate from narrator-level verification.
**Chapter 33:** Mary Magdalene maps Sophia's first repentance to Psalm 68 (Mead numbering). This is a second-order interpretive observer, not Sophia's own speech.
**Chapter 36:** Peter interprets the second repentance and challenges Mary's speaking prominence. Observer position includes social access to the interpretive forum.
**Chapter 57:** Sophia explicitly connects twelve earlier repentances to twelve aeons and the thirteenth to the thirteenth aeon. The count is textually grounded; a physical resonance mechanism is not.
**Chapters 58–60:** Narration introduces a rescuing light-power, a wreath, and converging light-powers. These are *narrative* developments after the thirteenth repentance, not laboratory observations.
**Chapters 60–62:** Several interpreters (Mary Magdalene, Mary mother of Jesus, and others) supply different correspondences for the same scriptural images, with Jesus affirming their explanations. This offers a particularly strong textual test case for multi-observer, multi-mapping interpretation, without presuming all readings are identical.
**Chapter 62:** The transmitted English includes a scribal note with letter/syllable strings. Do not infer Coptic phonetics or sound frequencies from Mead's English rendering; examine manuscript and editorial apparatus first.
