## Evidence-led corrections
- Rescue/assistance starts after repentance 9 (ch. 52), not only after repentance 13.
- Repentance 8 spans chapters 47–48 and contains renewed oppression within the same repentance. Therefore repentance count != state-change count.
- Repentance 7 (ch. 46) includes Sophia checking whether her repentance has been accepted; the narrative reports it has not yet been. Distinguish Sophia's observation of mocking from the narrator's stated reason.
- In ch. 52 Sophia's identification of the compassionate light contrasts with earlier mistaken attribution of a lion-faced power. This is the strongest candidate for testing observer-reference discrimination; do not reduce it to intensity alone, since compassion is also an explicit cue.
- Interpretive authority is distributed across Mary, Peter, Martha, John, Philip, Andrew, Thomas, Matthew, James and Salome; the narrator's approval of interpretations is a separate layer.

## Formalization (proposal)
Represent event as (observer, state, perception, attributed referent, interpretation, speech capacity, action, other observers, narrated response, provenance). Keep first-person report, reported speech, and narrator's statement distinct. Use first-class unresolved holes for uncertain agent identities and references.

## Hypotheses and disconfirmations
H1: Every repentance updates reference attribution. **Not demonstrated**; many are petitions rather than explicit reidentifications.
H2: Narrative state evolves cumulatively. **Partially supported**, but repeated oppression and reversals preclude monotonic improvement.
H3: Observer capacity and interpretation co-vary. **Plausible**, but text does not measure cognition or physiology.
H4: Multiple observers provide different mappings. **Directly supported** by interpreter structure, but does not imply every interpretation is incompatible.
