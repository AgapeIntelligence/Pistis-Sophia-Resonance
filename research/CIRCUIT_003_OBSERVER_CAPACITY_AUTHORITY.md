## Source-derived observations
1. **Ch. 32, Sophia's first repentance:** Sophia reports seeing a lower light and thinking she could obtain it. She descends, encounters opposition, and says her voice cannot reach out of the darkness. She reports looking upward and seeing the rulers of the aeons watching and rejoicing. She explicitly describes having believed the lion-faced light-power belonged to the Light she trusted. Thus perception, inference, action, vocal access, external observers and retrospective attribution are distinct narrative elements.
2. **Ch. 33, Mary's interpretation:** Mary Magdalene interprets Sophia's first repentance through Psalm 68 in Mead's numbering. Her interpretation is a separate observation/reading, not Sophia's own statement.
3. **Ch. 34, validation:** Jesus praises Mary's interpretation. The narrative introduces a distinct layer of interpretive authorization.
4. **Ch. 35, Sophia's second repentance:** She appeals not to be abandoned in darkness, asks to be carried to higher aeons, and reports that adversaries say the Light has forsaken her. Crucially, an adversary's claim about her abandonment is not identical to Sophia's own belief or to an established narrator-level fact.
5. **Ch. 36, Peter:** Peter objects to Mary's frequency of speaking, and then offers an interpretation of Sophia's second repentance through Psalm 70 (Mead numbering). Access to speaking and authority to interpret are explicitly part of the frame.
6. **Ch. 37:** Jesus affirms Peter's interpretation and introduces Sophia's third repentance. This supports comparing repeated petitions with evolving narrative/interpretive context, but not yet a physical phase mechanism.

## Analytical proposal (NOT an ancient formula)
For observer i at narrative step t, keep separate: available signals P(i,t), attributed referents R(i,t), interpretation I(i,t), action A(i,t), expression capacity V(i,t), and attributed authority G(i,t). Avoid treating them as independent physical dimensions or measuring them without an operational definition.

## Revised hypothesis set
- H1: Sophia's reference attribution changes in explicit, text-identifiable ways.
- H2: Her expressive and action capacities vary independently of reference accuracy.
- H3: The interpretations by Mary and Peter are a second-order observer layer, with social conditions of speaking.
- H4: The thirteen repentances comprise a cumulative observer transformation rather than simply repeated petitions. **Not yet established.**

## Counterchecks
- Distinguish narrator's voice, Sophia's reported speech, hostile rulers' speech, disciples' interpretations and Jesus's authorization.
- An utterance reporting another's claim does not validate that claim.
- A repeated prayer is not automatically a distinct reference correction.
- No acoustic frequency, phase-locking, or physiological mechanism can be concluded from the English translation.
