Status: experimental protocol and predictions, **not experimental results**. Date: 2026-11-05.

## Distinct objects
1. Manuscript graphemes and normalized tokens.
2. Proposed phoneme sequence(s) with uncertainty and citation.
3. Real speech recording(s) by identified consenting speakers; microphone and room documented.
4. Acoustic feature measurements (F0, formants, spectral centroid, bandwidth, envelope, rhythm, voiced/unvoiced segments).
5. Medium response measured with fixed membrane/plate geometry, tension, excitation, water depth where applicable, camera and illumination.
6. Observer report (speaker/listener interpretation), kept separate from physical measurements.

## Baseline model
Sound pressure x(t), spectrum X(f)=FFT[x(t)]; resonant-medium displacement y(r,t)=H_medium[x(t), geometry, boundary conditions]. A visible pattern is a function of sound and apparatus; it is not uniquely determined by a word's meaning or spelling.

## Proposed experiments
A. Record 10 repetitions of each selected utterance from the same speaker, fixed distance/room, comfortable volume. Include silent baseline and reference pure tones; avoid forced breathing or intense sound.
B. Measure duration, RMS level, F0 where voiced, formant trajectories, spectrogram, modulation spectrum and repeatability. Normalize level and compare within-speaker and across-speaker.
C. Compare carefully documented alternate pronunciation reconstructions. Mark the pronunciation as reconstruction, not an authentic surviving ancient recording.
D. Cymatics: play back the recorded utterances through a controlled transducer at calibrated safe level, alongside matched-level pure tones, broadband noise and reversed audio. Record membrane/plate response, lighting and camera conditions.
E. Blind pattern comparison: ask whether observers can distinguish words above chance once level, duration, speaker, and apparatus effects are controlled. Pre-register measures.
F. Test repeatability across sessions and transducers; report uncertainty and negative results.

## Predictions / falsifiers
- Speech's changing spectrum may generate dynamic medium patterns, especially around apparatus resonances.
- The same word may yield different patterns across speakers or pronunciations; a stable word-specific fingerprint is **not presumed**.
- If a pattern vanishes after amplitude equalization, it is likely driven by level rather than phonetic structure.
- If matched spectral envelopes generate the same pattern irrespective of lexical meaning, semantic-specific cymatic claims are unsupported.
- Claims of encoded truth, deception, consciousness or spiritual states in cymatic shapes require independent, blinded validation; no such result is asserted.

## Observer-provenance record
For each observation: recording ID; speaker and consent; pronunciation model; session state (self-report only); microphone/transducer; environment; medium configuration; processing steps; blind scorer; uncertainties; data access permissions.
